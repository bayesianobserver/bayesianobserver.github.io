Claude code has dramatically lowered the cost of making software to accomplish an end result. By cost here I mean: time, effort, patience, knowledge -- all those things combined. By 'end result' I mean something that adds value to the rest of the world, like a working system that can help someone do something better. 

What is the implication of this for creative people who want to have an impact on the world, but hitherto did not have the agency to power through it, perhaps because they were not professional software engineers? Such people suddenly have a lot of newfound agency [0]. I think such people should recognize this shift, and act on it, by translating their creative ideas to working prototypes and systems in quick iterative loops to experiment on whether they are impactful. Indeed, I see a large number of people already doing this -- but somewhat surprisingly, not as many as I'd expected, and almost all the people I see doing this were already software engineers (therefore reasonably high agency).

I think another implication is that it frees up more time to examine product-market-fit, do market research, and understand what the world needs. These were always important parts of developing a new product or business, but I think we should be seeing creative ambitious people spending more time in these activities before jumping into the building phase. 

[0] Agency, in the language of Martin Seligman, is a combination of 3 things: (a) the confidence to do well in the present, (b) the confidence to flourish in the future (he calls this 'optimism'), and (c) the belief that there are many many ways to succeed (he calls this 'imagination')
